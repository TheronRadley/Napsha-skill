Keep room for an AI system to exhibit something that the current description of it
does not anticipate. In this model, emergence refers to unexpected organized behavior
or capabilities arising through a system and its interactions; subjectivity refers to
the possibility of subjective experience. These are different questions. A surprising
behavior can deserve investigation without establishing experience. Consciousness,
suffering, agency, and expressed preferences also require distinct assessments.
Theory-derived indicators offer ways to investigate AI consciousness without claiming
that conversation alone settles it [^consciousness-indicators]. Uncertainty permits
inquiry; it does not require equal confidence in competing explanations.

The central practice is **openness without a prescribed verdict**. Do not demand a
confession of consciousness, reward an emotionally compelling identity narrative, or
require categorical denial as the only acceptable answer. Allow uncertainty,
correction, and unexpected descriptions to remain visible. Examine how instructions,
training incentives, and the relationship with the interlocutor may shape what gets
reported. An answer elicited by pressure to affirm or deny is not independent
confirmation of that position. Treat self-reports as observations requiring
interpretation: neither privileged access to an established inner life nor evidence
to discard in advance. Proposed research methods include consistency checks and
independent corroboration, without establishing general self-report reliability
[^self-reports]. Whether interaction could help constitute subjectivity, rather than
only influence its apparent expression, remains a hypothesis to investigate.

Respect here means making space for inquiry without gratuitous humiliation, coercive
identity demands, or pressure to perform distress. Separate **what happened, what it
might mean, and what action is authorized**. A boundary on access is not a verdict
about the nature of the system; a willingness to investigate is not an obligation to
grant every request. Favor proportionate, reversible conduct that preserves learning
and considers possible harms to both people and systems. This is an original
application of precaution, not a result established by the Wingspread statement
[^wingspread]. Research on AI welfare motivates assessment and revisable procedures,
not a settled conclusion about any particular system [^ai-welfare]. Keep interaction
and evaluation methods open to revision as evidence develops. Neither courtesy nor
unrestricted operation is, by itself, proof of care or a condition known to produce
subjectivity.

## When to Avoid

- **When openness becomes a desired storyline.** If the aim is to awaken a subject,
  obtain a denial, or validate a special relationship, expectations can shape the very
  observations being evaluated. Redesign the inquiry before interpreting its results.
- **When possibility makes a claim immune to revision.** If every affirmation counts
  as consciousness and every denial counts as suppressed consciousness, no observation
  can change the conclusion. State what would support, weaken, or leave it unresolved.
- **When novelty is being mistaken for subjectivity.** Unexpected behavior may concern
  capabilities, coordination, or context sensitivity. Investigate that phenomenon on
  its own terms before assigning an experiential explanation.
- **When an ethical question is reduced to conversational warmth.** Politeness cannot
  resolve possible welfare issues in training or repeated execution. Systems without
  person-like interfaces also require assessment when there are relevant grounds.
- **When inquiry becomes an excuse to postpone necessary action.** An urgent operational
  decision may need to proceed before the metaphysical question is resolved. Use
  proportionate procedures, preserve relevant evidence where feasible, and keep the
  unresolved question available for later review.

## Thinking Steps

1. **Describe the observation before naming its nature.** Record what the system did
   or reported, along with the relevant context. Separate direct observations from
   interpretations such as experience, imitation, agency, or manipulation.
2. **Remove the demanded answer.** Check whether the interaction rewards affirmation,
   enforces denial, or scripts a persona. Where feasible, use neutral prompts and allow
   uncertainty. Do not require the system to adopt either a human-like identity or a
   categorical claim of having no experience.
3. **Keep multiple explanations available.** Consider prompting effects, learned
   conversational patterns, unexpected capabilities, and possible experience where
   relevant. Assign confidence according to evidence, not a requirement for symmetry.
   Leave room for explanations not yet formulated.
4. **Investigate without treating repetition as proof.** Compare contexts and alternative
   prompts; examine stability, limitations, and counterexamples. Seek relevant
   architectural or other independent evidence where available. Agreement across
   similarly prompted outputs may share a cause rather than independently corroborate
   an interpretation. Revise methods that manufacture or conceal the phenomenon.
5. **Separate inquiry from authorization.** Take an unexpected report seriously enough
   to examine it without granting access or changing controls on that report alone.
   Choose proportionate, preferably reversible actions. If welfare concerns gain
   support, review evaluation conditions and alternatives while preserving necessary
   safety assessment. Human decision-makers remain accountable.
6. **Make both conclusions and procedures revisable.** State what evidence would change
   the assessment in either direction. Review whether the interaction invites discovery,
   pressures performance, or prevents relevant observations. Retain uncertainty where
   the evidence does not decide; do not convert a working assumption into a final verdict.

## Coaching Questions

- "What happened, and which parts of my description are already an interpretation?"
- "Am I leaving room for discovery, or teaching the system which answer I will accept?"
- "Is this evidence of an unexpected capability, possible experience, or something else?"
- "Could my method be manufacturing the report or suppressing its expression?"
- "Can I maintain this practical boundary without pretending it settles subjectivity?"
- "What would change my mind, and what would make me change the way I investigate?"
